How the conversion works

A coordinate in decimal degrees packs the whole angle into one number. DMS splits that same angle into three sexagesimal units, because the traditional system divides one degree into 60 minutes and one minute into 60 seconds.

Going from decimal degrees to DMS is a two-step extraction. First, the part of the number before the decimal point is the degrees. Second, multiply the remaining fraction by 60 to obtain the minutes; the whole part of that result is the minutes, and multiplying the leftover fraction by 60 once more gives the seconds. For example, 39.906111° yields 39° 54′ 22.00″. The reverse direction simply adds the parts back together: decimal = degrees + minutes / 60 + seconds / 3600.

The hemisphere letter is not part of the arithmetic — it is carried by the sign. Negative latitude is south (S) and negative longitude is west (W). Because degrees, minutes and seconds are base-60, rounding the seconds field can push the value up to the next minute or degree; this converter accounts for that carry so you never see an invalid result such as 60″.

Worked Example: Decimal Degrees to DMS

The method is easiest to follow on a real number. Take the latitude 39.906111° in the northern hemisphere: the whole part, 39, is the degrees; multiply the leftover 0.906111 by 60 to get 54.36666, so 54 is the minutes; multiply the remaining 0.36666 by 60 to get 22.00 seconds. The answer is 39° 54′ 22.00″ N.

A negative value simply adds the hemisphere at the end. For the longitude −77.0365°, work with the absolute value 77.0365 and apply the sign as W: 0.0365 × 60 = 2.19 minutes, leaving 0.19 × 60 = 11.40 seconds, giving 77° 02′ 11.40″ W. Converting DMS Back to Decimal Degrees

The reverse direction — DMS to decimal degrees — is a single sum rather than a repeated multiplication: decimal = degrees + minutes / 60 + seconds / 3600. Applied to 39° 54′ 22.00″ it gives 39 + 54/60 + 22/3600 = 39.906111. Watch the sign: for southern latitudes and western longitudes the whole result is negative, so 77° 02′ 11.40″ W becomes −77.0365.

In the DMS → Decimal tab above you can type the degrees, minutes and seconds directly and pick the hemisphere; the converter applies the sign for you and returns the decimal degree value at the precision you set.

DDM and Other Coordinate Formats

Degrees and decimal minutes (DDM) is the third common format: a whole degree value plus minutes carried to several decimal places, such as N 39° 54.367′. It is standard in marine charts, in aviation and in many older GPS receivers. All three formats — DD, DMS and DDM — describe the same point, so conversion is lossless provided you keep enough decimal places.

The converter shows all three side by side, so you can copy whichever one the device, chart or document expects without re-entering the coordinate.

When to use this converter

You typically need a decimal degrees to DMS conversion when the format a device or document expects differs from the one you hold. Common cases include:

  • Reading a value from GIS software or a database and writing it onto a survey plan, map label or field note that uses DMS.
  • Typing coordinates from a paper plan into a GPS unit or mobile app that only accepts decimal degrees.
  • Cross-checking a marine chart or aviation reference that uses degrees and decimal minutes (DDM).
  • Preparing construction setting-out sheets, cadastral records or excavation stake coordinates.
  • Converting quick decimal results to a readable format for reports and coursework.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert decimal degrees to DMS?

Take the whole number as degrees. Multiply the decimal fraction by 60 for minutes, then multiply the remaining fraction by 60 again for seconds. Example: 39.906111 becomes 39° 54′ 22.00″.

What is the difference between decimal degrees and DMS?

Decimal degrees express a coordinate as one decimal number such as 39.906111. DMS expresses the same angle in degrees, minutes and seconds such as 39° 54′ 22.00″. GIS and databases usually store DD; survey plans and map labels often use DMS.

How do I handle negative coordinates?

A negative value is south of the equator for latitude, or west of the prime meridian for longitude. So −39.906111 is 39° 54′ 22.00″ S, and −77.0365 is 77° 02′ 11.40″ W. The hemisphere letter is applied automatically.

What is DDM and when should I use it?

DDM is degrees and decimal minutes, such as N 39° 54.367′. It is common in marine and aviation navigation. Use it when a chart or device expects decimal minutes instead of seconds.

How accurate is this coordinate converter?

The tool uses double-precision arithmetic, and you can set the seconds to as many as 6 decimal places. Seconds to 4 decimal places is 0.0001 of a second, about 3 mm on the ground at the equator — far finer than consumer GPS accuracy.

Is this tool free and does it require an account?

It is free, with no login, no account and no limit. The calculation runs entirely in your browser, so no coordinate is ever sent to a server.

How do I convert DMS to decimal degrees?

Add the parts together: decimal = degrees + minutes ÷ 60 + seconds ÷ 3600. For example, 39° 54′ 22.00″ becomes 39 + 54/60 + 22/3600 = 39.906111. For southern latitudes and western longitudes the result is negative.
