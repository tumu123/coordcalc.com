How the calculation works

Slope describes how much a surface rises for a given horizontal distance. Given a rise (vertical change) and a run (horizontal distance), the three common expressions of the same gradient are related by simple trigonometry:

  • Percent slope = rise ÷ run × 100 — the rise over run ratio written as a percentage. A rise of 1 over a run of 12 is 8.33%.
  • Angle in degrees = arctan(rise ÷ run), converted from radians by multiplying by 180 ÷ π.
  • Ratio = 1 : n, where n = run ÷ rise. The same 1 in 12 case gives 1 : 12.

Roof pitch is the rise measured over a fixed run of 12, written as x:12 — a convention from imperial construction practice. It is therefore just the percent slope recalculated against a run of 12, and a 1:12 slope is identical to a 1:12 roof pitch.

The slope length is the diagonal distance along the inclined surface. By Pythagoras' theorem it equals √(rise² + run²). Note that percent slope and slope length use different denominators: percent slope is based on the horizontal run, not on the distance along the slope, which is a frequent source of error in quantity take-offs.

To convert degrees to percent slope or percent slope to degrees, the tool applies the inverse relationship: percent = tan(angle) × 100 and angle = arctan(percent ÷ 100). A 45-degree slope is therefore 100 percent, while an elevation grade of 5 percent is only about 2.86 degrees — the two units diverge quickly as the angle rises.

Rise Over Run: The Slope Percentage Formula

Rise over run is the simplest definition of a slope: divide the vertical change (the rise) by the horizontal distance (the run). The quotient is the gradient as a decimal, and multiplying it by 100 gives the percent slope. A rise of 1 with a run of 12 is 0.0833, so 8.33 percent. Using a rise over run calculator saves re-entry, because the same three numbers produce a percentage, an angle in degrees, a 1:n ratio and a roof pitch in x:12 at once.

One caution: the run is always measured horizontally. If you substitute the slope length — the distance measured along the inclined surface — the percentage comes out too low, and the error grows with steepness. This is the most frequent mistake in drainage falls and in quantity take-offs.

Roof Pitch Calculator (x:12)

A roof pitch calculator reports the same gradient in the units roofers use: the rise measured over a fixed run of 12, written as x:12. A 4:12 pitch rises 4 units for every 12 units of horizontal run, which is the same as 18.43 degrees or a 33.3 percent slope. Residential roofs commonly sit between 4:12 and 9:12; below 4:12 the roof is treated as low-slope and needs more careful waterproofing, and 2:12 is often quoted as the practical minimum for asphalt shingles.

Because pitch, degrees and percent all describe one angle, the calculator shows all three together, so you can match whichever figure appears on the drawing, the material datasheet or the building code.

Ramp Slope and the ADA 1:12 Rule

Ramps are where slope percentage becomes a legal limit rather than a convenience. The ADA standard in the United States caps the running slope of a wheelchair ramp at 1:12 — one unit of rise for every 12 units of run — which equals 8.33 percent or 4.76 degrees. A ramp slope calculator lets you check a layout against that ceiling: enter the total rise and the available run, and a result above 8.33 percent tells you the ramp needs a longer run, a landing or a switchback.

The same 1:12 reference applies to kerb ramps and accessible routes, while a slope of 1:20 (5 percent) or flatter is generally treated as a walkway rather than a ramp. Always confirm the current edition of the governing code, since thresholds and exceptions are updated periodically.

Excavation and Trench Slope

In excavation, slope is a safety control. An excavation slope calculator checks whether a cut batter is stable for the soil present: firm clay or rock may stand at 1:1 (45 degrees, 100 percent), while loose sand, gravel or previously disturbed fill usually needs 1.5:1 (about 34 degrees, 67 percent) or flatter. Many jurisdictions require a protective system — sloping, shoring or a trench box — once a trench reaches 5 feet (1.5 m) in depth.

Enter the depth and the horizontal offset to get the working slope, then verify it against the applicable excavation standard, because the permitted batter depends on soil classification, moisture content and how long the excavation will stay open.

Frequently asked questions

How do you calculate slope percentage?

Percent slope equals rise ÷ run × 100. A rise of 1 metre over a run of 12 metres is 8.33%.

How do you convert percent slope to degrees?

Angle = arctan(percent ÷ 100). So 100% becomes arctan(1) = 45°. In reverse, percent = tan(angle) × 100.

What does a 1:12 slope mean?

One unit of rise for every 12 units of run — 8.33% or 4.76°. It is the standard ratio form for ramps and drainage, and equals a 1:12 roof pitch.

What is roof pitch and how is it written?

Roof pitch is the rise over 12 units of run, written x:12. A 4:12 pitch rises 4 units for every 12 units of horizontal run.

How do you calculate slope length?

Slope length = √(rise² + run²), by Pythagoras' theorem — the diagonal distance along the slope, which is what you need when ordering roofing, pipe or fencing.

What is considered a steep slope?

As a general guide: under 2% nearly level, 2–5% gentle, 5–10% moderate, 10–20% steep, above 20% very steep. Always check the applicable design standard, as acceptable limits vary by application.

How do I use a slope percentage calculator?

Enter the rise and the horizontal run in the same unit. The slope percentage calculator divides the rise by the run and multiplies by 100, then shows the same gradient as an angle, a 1:n ratio and a roof pitch in x:12. Measure the run horizontally, not along the sloping surface.

What slope is required for an ADA ramp?

The ADA standard caps a ramp's running slope at 1:12 — 8.33% or 4.76°. A slope of 1:20 (5%) or flatter is treated as a walkway rather than a ramp. Confirm the current edition of the governing code, as thresholds are updated periodically.

What is rise over run, and what is elevation grade?

Rise over run is the vertical rise divided by the horizontal run — a slope of 1 in 4, written 1:4. Multiply by 100 to get the elevation grade: a rise of 1 over a run of 4 is 25 percent. The same gradient is 14.04 degrees and a roof pitch of 3:12.
